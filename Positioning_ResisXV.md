## 1. Golden Circle

**Why:** Keeping a job should leave enough time to recover and maintain life at home. [H: brand belief informed by I1 and contrasting household accounts in I3.]

**How:** Make an occasional night near work a practical choice by aligning acceptable accommodation, authorized employer support and a personal cost the worker accepts. [H: proposed approach.]

**What:** An employer-supported lodging service for occasional nights near work. [H: proposed category and offer.]

The aim is recovery between workdays. A stay is one possible response, and returning home can remain the better choice.

## 2. Positioning statement (Moore)

For Mexico City workers with long commutes who need rest between workdays, ResisXV is an employer-supported lodging service for occasional nights near work. Unlike arranging lodging alone, it would make the worker’s personal cost and company support clear before a stay is confirmed. [H]

This carries the PRD Vision forward unchanged. It names the user, job, category, alternative and proposed benefit in fewer than 50 words. The benefit is a service promise to test, not an exclusive capability or demonstrated competitive advantage.

## 5. Messaging pillars and evidence

### Pillar 1: Recover Time

**Message:** Explore a night near work when the journey leaves little time to recover. [H]

**Evidence:** I1 reports about two hours outbound and late returns. The MRD supports travel burden in three worker records, while actual sleep gain remains unknown.

**Proposed support:** suitable stay hours, confirmed access and voluntary rest diaries. Do not promise a specific number of hours saved or improved work attendance.

### Pillar 2: Clear Support

**Message:** Know the company contribution and your personal cost before committing. [H]

**Evidence:** I1 cannot self-fund; I2 raises a company-wide MXN 3,000–4,000 weekly funding range and concerns about allocation. Interest is not authorization, and that amount does not apply per worker.

**Proposed support:** authorized policy, accepted quote, visible copay and accountable company spending. The company commitment must include any employer-paid coordination fee, as specified in the PRD.

### Pillar 3: Choose Freely

**Message:** Choose one night when it fits your work and home. [H]

**Evidence:** I1 and I3 describe different household circumstances. Their accounts do not support a routine-stay assumption for all commuters.

**Proposed support:** one-night choice, optional household information, clear cancellation consequences and no automatic self-pay fallback when support is denied.

## 6. Tagline

**Working selection: One night near work, with employer support.** [H]

This names the occasion and funding model without promising cheaper lodging or a measured rest benefit. It is recommended for the draft; no user selection or messaging test has occurred.

Alternatives considered:

- **Rest near work. Keep your home.** Emphasizes the outcome and household continuity, but leaves the funding mechanism unclear. [H]
- **A nearby night, with clear support.** Emphasizes clarity, but does not identify the employer as clearly. [H]

A direct provider could adopt employer support, and a company could coordinate stays manually. The tagline therefore describes the intended offer; it does not establish a moat or an exclusive market position.

## 8. Open questions for the marketing website and PRD

1. **Who is the authorized buyer?** Confirm authority, budget period, company-wide cap, allocation rules and who pays the provider and coordination fee.
2. **Can an acceptable offer fit the fund and personal cap?** Obtain two matched all-in quotes and real worker choices before publishing prices or savings claims.
3. **Does the stay improve recovery enough to repeat?** Use the PRD diary and outcome definitions. Do not infer sleep or attendance gains from travel estimates.
4. **Which occasions fit the household?** Test voluntary use rather than assuming routine weekday stays.
5. **What beats direct booking or a manual coordinator?** Measure the extra approval effort, clarity and total operating cost. Keep the simpler service if it works better.
6. **What can the website claim now?** Present a proposed employer-supported pilot, not an operating booking marketplace. Use an employer pilot-discussion call to action; do not imply guaranteed worker eligibility, room availability or a live contact form unless implemented.
7. **Which wording should be retained?** Test the working tagline and messages with workers and an authorized buyer. The copy is drafted; no tagline preference or message comprehension result is recorded.

Current assessment: **Proficient as a positioning draft; differentiation remains conditional.** The first row to strengthen is the differentiated benefit through a matched real alternative and authorized funding. The marketing website should use sections 2, 4, 5 and 6, retaining the pilot status and limits above.
