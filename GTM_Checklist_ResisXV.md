## The eight business questions

### 1. Value creation and does positioning justify it?

The unit of value is one completed, reconciled supported stay, with personal cost accepted in advance and usable arrival arrangements. The intended worker benefit is more time to recover without a permanent move. The employer receives control of a limited commitment and a record of actual spending. Rest gain, repeat demand and employer value remain unmeasured. [H]

The positioning supports this unit: occasional near-work accommodation with clear company support and personal cost. It does not justify claims of cheaper lodging, guaranteed safety or lower absenteeism.

### 2. Value capture model

Test an employer-paid coordination fee per completed stay. Lodging is charged separately at the agreed provider price and is not platform revenue. The PRD’s MXN 50 fee is illustrative, not approved pricing. [H]

The working company cap includes both lodging subsidy and the coordination fee. Identify the payment recipient, timing, cancellation liability and worker copay before any commitment. No card custody, automatic payroll deduction or worker deposits are specified for R1.

### 3. Tiering

Start with one pilot service, not subscription tiers: one worksite, agreed stay hours, a defined funded period and named operating contacts. Do not publish a public nightly price or introductory discount before a buyer accepts the model and suitable providers quote the actual stay. [H]

### 4. Add-on value

The core includes funding clarity, provider confirmation, arrival contact, cancellation terms and reconciliation. R2 may add richer travel/cost comparisons and structured issue/dispute tools. R3 may add aggregate review and renewal. These are planned improvements, not paid add-ons or available capabilities. [H]

### 5. Repositioning cadence

Review the promise at Week 6 readiness and Week 12 reassessment, then after each funded period. Compare refusals, rest outcomes, coordination effort and direct alternatives. If manual coordination meets the need more simply, narrow the offer. Do not change published claims without updating their evidence and the affected PRD requirement. [H]

### 6. Channels and key partners

Begin with direct conversations with an authorized employer, followed by voluntary worker recruitment through that worksite. The website and prototype explain the proposal; they do not replace authorization. Required partners are a funding employer and suitable accommodation providers, supported by a responsible coordinator. None is confirmed. Public provider examples are not partnerships. [H]

**Qualified employer lead:** a relevant worksite, a reachable funding decision maker and willingness to discuss budget, allocation and operating conditions. Track contacted employers → qualified conversations → written pilot agreements → funded periods. No funnel conversion rate or acquisition forecast is established.

### 7. How we show off

Demonstrate the existing worker and employer wireframes: choose a night, review support/cost, request, distinguish pending from confirmed, arrange arrival and reconcile. Identify static demo data clearly. Demonstrate denial and cancellation as well as the happy path.

Use the three pillars **Recover Time, Clear Support and Choose Freely**. Present the generated persona portrait as an illustration. Do not use drafted perceptions as testimonials or present target metrics as achieved outcomes.

### 8. CAC, LTV, payback

Observed CAC, LTV and payback are **Open**. The PRD uses the following sensitivity example, all in MXN: [H]

| Input / calculation | Planning scenario |
|---|---|
| Employer outreach cost | 1,600–3,200 per acquired employer, assuming one acquisition; actual conversion unknown. |
| Fee revenue per stay | 50. |
| Variable cost at 15 minutes × 120/hour, plus 5 allowance | 35; contribution = 15 per stay. |
| At 40 completed stays/month | 600 monthly contribution before fixed costs. |
| Outreach-only payback before fixed costs | 1,600–3,200 ÷ 600 = approximately 2.7–5.3 months. This is not full CAC payback. |
| Slower coordination at 30 minutes/stay | Variable cost = 65; contribution = −15 per stay. |
| LTV / CAC | Not computable without observed renewal, retention, full acquisition costs and sustainable margin. |

The PRD’s total pre-build, pre-lodging pilot setup allowance is 6,600–13,200. Monthly infrastructure of 600–1,500 and maintenance of 1,200–4,800 are additional planning costs; staffed arrival coverage is unpriced. None is an approved budget. Positive per-stay contribution alone does not establish a viable business.

## Open items for the PRD

1. **Buyer and fund:** verify the authority, period, company-wide cap and treatment of coordination fees. Owner: sponsor lead. Blocks live commitments.
2. **Supply and worker acceptance:** obtain two matched offers and accepted personal limits for actual occasions. Owner: operations and PM. Blocks price and availability promises.
3. **Payment and cancellation:** specify who pays whom, when, and who bears each cancellation or disputed charge. Owner: finance and operations. Blocks paid stays.
4. **Operating capacity and economics:** name the support owner, price required coverage and replace assumed handling costs with actual evidence. Owner: operations and finance. Blocks expansion and may block the pilot.
5. **Website readiness:** build the page, implement a real enquiry destination and agree privacy/contact information. Owner: PM and web implementer. Blocks a functional public call to action.
6. **Validation and continuation:** run usability sessions and the voluntary rest/cost test. Owner: PM. At W12, missing sample minimums are insufficient evidence, not a pass.
7. **PRD synchronization:** record approved commercial or operating changes in the existing PRD and decision log; update positioning and website claims when affected. Owner: PM. This checklist does not silently approve or alter those decisions.
